# Academic document design

The default template uses Calibri 11 pt, navy blue `#1F4E79` headings and black body text. A4 pages have 20 mm margins. Dates align to a right tab stop rather than a layout table. Native Word bullets, heading styles and readable hyperlinks preserve a straightforward reading order. Headers identify the candidate on later pages; footers show page counts. Headings stay with the following paragraph and citations stay with their DOI.

The faculty CV leads with the academic profile, education and teaching appointments, followed by research experience and publications. Research CVs bring research interests, research experience and publications ahead of teaching. Appointments use reverse chronology within each category. Submitted manuscripts have their own section, with the submitted venue and status stated explicitly. No academic record is upgraded by formatting.

Cover letters use a date, institution, role subject, salutation, a concise evidence-based argument and sign-off. The default drafting target is 350–450 words, subject to the listing's actual limits. Research and teaching statements use descriptive section headings. No table is forced into a narrative document; the CV uses paragraphs and lists for clarity.

The structure follows university careers guidance while the blue heading treatment follows the user's requested style:

- [Cambridge academic applications and interviews](https://www.careers.cam.ac.uk/academic-applications-and-interviews): academic CV content and role-specific research/teaching fit in covering letters.
- [Oxford Guide to Careers 2024](https://www.careers.ox.ac.uk/sitefiles/oxford-guide-careers-2024.pdf): relevant, concise, evidence-based academic records and academic-specific experience.

Template version 2 applies to newly generated documents. Saved packages keep their original versions until explicitly edited or regenerated. Layout is verified using Microsoft Word's exported PDFs and rendered page images, including both faculty and research ordering.
